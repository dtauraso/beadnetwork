Reconstructed from linksTree, stepRows, stepBody, headRow, linkCard and pressSummary. The values come from a typical sequence: 3 nodes, 2 steps.
  1. 1
    Four scopes share one scroll. The frame (ρ, T_φ, T_θ, c_r, Δt) reloads the scene when it changes, s belongs to the sequence, the network is "what runs now", and steps are the sequence's content. The only thing that tells them apart is a fold title. high
  2. 2
    The same link appears up to N + 2 times: under network, under start, and under every step. Seeing what 2→1 does across the whole sequence means opening each of them. high
  3. 3
    Only one step is open at a time. stepOpen is true only for the step shown, so comparing step 1 and step 2 means toggling back and forth. Nested steps hide behind → and a breadcrumb. high
  4. 4
    Arithmetic instead of a picture. "φ 3 + total 2 + 1 → tick 6" asks you to add numbers on a 0–12 wrap-around circle in your head, where 11 + 1 is 0. high
  5. 5
    Rules are only learned when they refuse. "One picked link into a node", "links out of a node share nΔ" and "fixed radius" all surface as text at the bottom of the list, far from the field that caused them. medium
  6. 6
    HEAD locks are unlabeled glyphs. ·, ↑ and ↓ cycle on click, with no word for what a lock does to the StepAll. medium
  7. 7
    Three numbers look alike but edit differently. Δ is a press field (±, not typed), nΔ is typed, and total is read-only. Nothing in their appearance says which is which. medium
  8. 8
    Navigation is explained by a 15-word hint, and → means three things: open, go in, and the button that enters nested steps. low
What already works and should be kept: the pick glyph's meaning, the per-step HEAD locks, nested steps, renaming in place, row drag to reorder, and keyboard navigation. The redesign changes how these are shown, not what they do.
