Step tree

Everything is a step, and every step can hold steps. Sequences stop being their own kind: they become the scene’s top-level steps. One outline of that tree replaces the step chips, “Go inside”, the trail and the Sequences page.

Model

A step is the only unit. The scene holds steps; a step holds steps; there is no depth limit and no row of another kind. Nodes are not part of the hierarchy.

Two storage facts sit under that model and stay out of the tree:

  • A top-level step carries the node set. Each one keeps its own node places, cards and view, as a sequence does now, and every step under it uses that set. Moving between two top-level steps therefore reloads the network (the dashed line). Moving anywhere inside one is in-process: loadGrid(level) at Categories/NodeKinds/PhiTheta/grid.go:82.
  • A step’s column lives in its parent. Opening step n shows the parent’s columns with n picked. The scene has no node set of its own, so the top level has no columns. Opening a top-level step shows its own step 1, or the step last shown in it.

What it reverses

  • Sequences as a kind. Today they have their own folder (sequences/), ids (#10), ops (seq-add, seq-copy, …), menu and page. After, a former sequence is a top-level step and uses the step ops, step names and step menu.
  • Navigating by descent. Today you see one level at a time, enter with “Go inside” and climb out with the trail. After, every level is visible at once, and where you are is the highlighted row.
  • The step chip bar as the step list. stepBar shows one level as a flat list. After, the tree is the step list. The “showing step N” status line stays.
The step columns (the score) and the inspector do not change. The node network does not change either: it already takes every path relative to the open sequence folder (NodesRoot), and that folder only moves.

Order

One commit per step, each with stop-checks clean and driven in the editor before the next.

  1. Move the data. migrate-top-steps plus the Scenes, Startup and readscene renames. The old UI keeps working, now reading steps/. Check with readscene before and after on every top step: identical output.
  2. One set of step ops. The step ops work at the top level, and the sequence ops become calls to them. The Sequences page still draws, as a view of the top level.
  3. Rows, then the pane. Build the rows and draw the tree beside the old navigators, with clicks doing nothing yet.
  4. tree-go. Inside one top step first, then across top steps, restoring the place from open-step.bin.
  5. Step menu on every row, with “Add inner step” and the filter field.
  6. Remove everything marked “gone” in one commit, so two ways of moving never disagree.
  7. Narrow width: the path chip and popover.

Verification

There are no tests. Verification is driving the editor on topology-phi-theta, whose steps 1 and 8 already hold steps two levels deep.

  • After step 1: sequences/ is gone, steps/1–steps/11 exist, and every top step draws the same diagram as before.
  • The tree shows steps 1–11, and steps 1 and 8 open two levels deep, matching the folders.
  • From step 10 › step 1, click step 8 › step 1 › step 1. The diagram reloads, that row is highlighted, and the columns show that level.
  • Duplicate a top step and an inner step from the same menu. Both copies appear as the next row.
  • Delete the open top step. A neighbour opens, and .probe/go-errors.log stays empty.
  • grep -rn 'Sequence\|OpSeq\|OpStepEnter\|stepBar' Categories is empty after step 6.

Risks

  1. Moving user data. The scene folders are untracked, so the move has no git safety net. The program copies the scene to a backup next to it before it changes anything.
  2. Top-level ops are heavier. Adding or deleting a top step copies or removes a node set and reloads. It looks like the same click as on an inner step but costs a reload; the reload has to land on the right row.
  3. Width. A third pane squeezes the columns. The tree needs a cap (around 200px) and the narrow-width fold.
  4. Walking the disk. Rebuild the rows on structure ops and reloads only, not on every layout.
  5. The 200-line ceiling. links_score.go (189) and step_dirs.go (163) are near it; top-level ops likely need their own file.

Open questions

  • Opening a top-level step: show its step 1, or the step last shown in it? The proposal is last shown, stored in that step’s view/.
  • Dropping ids means a row’s number changes when a step before it is deleted, as inner steps already do. Is that fine, or should every step get an id?
  • Where does fold state live? The proposal is beside each step’s open.bin.
