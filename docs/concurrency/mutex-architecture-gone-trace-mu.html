gone · Trace.mu — deleted by giving the drain the only write, not by a smaller lock

Every emitting goroutine sent on a channel, taking no lock, even before the lock was deleted. What the mutex actually guarded was Breadcrumb's direct sink writes (it used to write sink/debugSink straight from the calling goroutine, bypassing t.ch) and surviving a Close() that ran while goroutines were still shutting down. Both were fixed at the source: Breadcrumb now sends on t.ch like every other event, so the drain goroutine is the sole writer; and shutdown became complete — every nodeMover.run/edgeMover.run and RunStdinReader is now covered by a WaitGroup, so nothing can call into Trace during Close(). The accumulator-can't-be-copied justification below was true but was never what the lock was doing in production — this was the most misread lock in the tree.

DELETED, NOT NARROWED. The accumulator-can't-be-copied claim above was true but was not what the lock guarded — in production nothing ever raced events (the drain was already sole writer, Events() had zero non-test callers, WriteJSONL ran after drain exited). The lock's real job was serializing Breadcrumb's direct sink writes and surviving an incomplete shutdown; fixing both removed it. The framing above it is still open: the event channel and the drain's single-writer ordering still exist, and the merged log is already only arrival order at the drain — scheduler order, not causal order.
