A node holding t and told a turns one slot at a time until it stops. Update rules gives that as two machines and the rule an arrival runs. None of that is needed to say what happens. Given the two numbers you can write down where the node ends and how many arrivals it takes, and the turning in between is arithmetic you never have to watch.

The two numbers, and the one between them

What there is

a node draws three arrows — its TILT, its normal a quarter turn on, and its bottom a half turn on. Only the first of those is t, and it is the only one this arithmetic moves. The other two are read off it (Vectors), so they follow without appearing here

a is the PARTNER'S NORMAL, not the partner's tilt — a node sends the quarter-turn-on arrow, never its own tilt. That quarter turn is already inside a, which is why the two arrangements below come out 6 apart rather than 0 and 6

so d = t − a is this node's tilt against the other node's normal: one arrow each, and the only two numbers on this page

both are counts of slots round a ring of 24, so both are ordinary integers

ℓ is how far apart they are the short way round, so ℓ never exceeds 12

How far it is from stopping

One number, off the quarter

a pair is set up for one of two arrangements, and each stops at its own angle: parallel at the quarter, perpendicular at either end

both are symmetric about the quarter, so q — how far ℓ sits off 6 — settles both, one way up or the other. The two f being complements is the same fact the audit found by drawing them

f is a DISTANCE, in slots, and it says nothing about WHICH WAY — ℓ folded the sign away to get it. The next card puts the sign back, and the direction comes with it

One round

Where it is after one arrival

ℓ above threw the SIGN away, which is why a rule built on it has to compare two neighbours to get direction back. Keep the signed gap and the direction is already there — it is the sign of e

the rests sit every 12 apart in d, so taking d modulo 12 asks "how far past the last rest", and subtracting 6 recentres that on the nearest one. The two arrangements differ by a SHIFT OF 6 INSIDE the modulus and by nothing else

d is NOT reduced first — not folded, not even brought onto the ring. 24 is a whole number of 12s, so every way of writing t − a gives the same e, and reducing it would be work the modulus immediately undoes

the two forms meet here: bring d onto the ring and its size IS ℓ. The magnitude the page opened with is this gap with its sign dropped — and dropping the sign is exactly what costs it the direction. e never needs that fold, which is why it keeps what ℓ lost

t_after is the TILT — the same one arrow. The normal and the bottom are read off it, so one subtraction here moves all three drawn arrows and nothing else needs updating

a tilt exactly between two rests is where a comparison rule has to be told what to prefer. Here it needs nothing: those land on e = −6 by themselves, and −6 turns up, which is what the code does. 96 of the 1152 pairs are such ties, so this is not a corner being skirted

The whole run, without running it

Where it stops, and when

f is not only how far off it is — it is the COUNT. Each arrival closes exactly one slot, so f arrivals is the whole run and there is no arrival number f + 1

s is −sign(e) from the card above, and it is decided once. The walk cannot turn back, which is why the landing slot is a multiplication and not a simulation

so f = 0 is not a special case or a halt condition — it is the run that takes no arrivals

What this does not say

It holds for a held arrival

a is treated here as fixed. In a live pair it is not: each end sends its own normal and both turn, so a moves while t does. The closed form describes one node against a direction that stays put

that is the honest limit of this page. The exchange itself — two ends, each turning on what the other last sent — is the run on Update rules, and it is why the code keeps the general form (a comparison per arrival) rather than the shortcut above

the arithmetic is still what the machine IS. It is not a model of the machine, and not the machine transcribed: it never mentions a state, and there is nothing in it to step
