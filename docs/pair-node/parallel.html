Four lines of arithmetic move: two sends lose their ∓12, two step rules swap their signs. Nothing else in the loop changes.

Why swapping the signs is exactly the right compensation

t + 12 ≡ t − 12 (mod 24) the two kinds' bottoms are ONE index. Reversing a direction is adding 12, and adding 12 twice returns it
d(v + 12, top) = d(v, top − 12) = d(v, bottom) reversing what is SENT is the same as testing the receiver's OTHER direction. The two tests trade places, and nothing else about them changes
{0…5, 19…23} + 12 = {7…17} acute maps onto obtuse against the SAME direction — which is why a node cannot simply keep its old rule and read the unreversed vector: every arrival that used to fire the top test now fires the bottom one
{6, 18} + 12 = {6, 18} the halt set is FIXED under reversal. Perpendicular to the top is perpendicular to the bottom, either way round — so the exchange stops on exactly the same arrivals it stops on today
The two edits therefore compose to the identity: every arrival produces the same index change it produces today, and the pair walks the same path to the same rest state. This is a change of what travels on the wire, not of what the pair does.

What this bought, and the one thing to watch

the wire says what it means a node sends the direction it actually computed and holds, so the message on the channel matches the arrow drawn on the sender
one fewer constant in the path the ∓12 existed only to be undone by the receiver's sign; the pair of them was a round trip through nothing
the sign stays with the kind every remaining per-kind sign is in one place, the step rule, instead of split across a send and a step
what to watch the third arrow. It is drawn from r, and r is what ARRIVED, so it now flips by 180° at rest compared to the reversed rule. If that arrow is read as "what my partner is aiming at me", unreversed is the more honest picture; if it is read as "the direction I was pushed toward", it now points away
