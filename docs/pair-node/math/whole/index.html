The triangle

A square of side \(b\) has a diagonal whose square is \(2b^2\). Stack \(k\) of them and the squares add to \(2k\,b^2\): one leg of a right triangle whose other leg is \(a\) and whose diagonal is \(c\).

\[ a^2 + 2k\,b^2 = c^2 \qquad (a, b, c) = 12n\,(x, y, z) \qquad x^2 + 2k\,y^2 = z^2 \]

Every side a multiple of \(12n\) is the same triangle scaled, so each row is one \((x, y, z)\).

The angles as ticks

On the card an angle is a point on the ring, and the ring's size is the common denominator of the angles in use: \(12\) to a turn, \(3\) to a quarter. A triangle's angle is a tick only when it is a fraction of a turn.

each ring cell is the angle at \(a\), then the angle at the stack: a sixth and a twelfth of a turn, so on a ring of \(R\) ticks they are \(R/6\) and \(R/12\), and they sum to a quarter turn, \(R/4\)

12 is the smallest ring that holds both; a bigger ring gives the same angles more ticks, and brings in no new triangle

searched in integers only — double the angle, square the denominator, reduce, and see whether the fraction comes back: every triangle with \(k \le 1000\), \(y \le 300\) (1,860,584 of them, \(z\) up to 45,000,001) lands only at \(t = 1 \ldots 12\); every triangle with \(k \le 3500\), \(y \le 60\) (1,192,835 of them, \(z\) up to 6,300,001) lands only at \(t = 1 \ldots 24\)
