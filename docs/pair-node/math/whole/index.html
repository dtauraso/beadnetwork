The equation as a count

Every term is a count of unit squares: \(a^2\) of them, plus \(2k\) blocks of \(b^2\), make \(c^2\).

\[ a^2 + 2k\,b^2 = c^2 \qquad (a, b, c) = 12n\,(x, y, z) \qquad x^2 + 2k\,y^2 = z^2 \]

Each row is one \((x, y, z)\) with no common factor; multiplying by \(12n\) gives the rest.

A direction is a pair

For one \(k\), a direction is a pair of whole numbers \((x, y)\). Two directions combine by one rule, and nothing in it divides or takes a root:

\[ (x_1, y_1) \cdot (x_2, y_2) = (x_1 x_2 - 2k\,y_1 y_2,\;\; x_1 y_2 + x_2 y_1) \]
  • same direction — \((x_1, y_1)\) and \((x_2, y_2)\) with \(x_1 y_2 = x_2 y_1\), signs matching: one is a whole-number multiple of the other's direction
  • half turn — a pair \((x, 0)\) with \(x < 0\)
  • full turn — a pair \((x, 0)\) with \(x > 0\)
  • quarter turn — \((0, 1)\), since \((0, 1) \cdot (0, 1) = (-2k, 0)\), a half turn
  • turned back — \((x, -y)\) undoes \((x, y)\), since \((x, y) \cdot (x, -y) = (x^2 + 2k\,y^2, 0)\), a full turn
  • ring of \(R\) — a base pair \(e\) whose \(R\)-th power is the first full turn; a direction is \(n\) ticks when it has the same direction as \(e^n\)

Each row's \((x, y)\) is multiplied by itself. The numbers grow every time; the question is only whether the second number ever reaches \(0\).
