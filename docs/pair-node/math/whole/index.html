The triangle

A square of side \(b\) has a diagonal whose square is \(2b^2\). Stack \(k\) of them and the squares add to \(2k\,b^2\): one leg of a right triangle whose other leg is \(a\) and whose diagonal is \(c\).

\[ a^2 + 2k\,b^2 = c^2 \qquad (a, b, c) = 12n\,(x, y, z) \qquad x^2 + 2k\,y^2 = z^2 \]

Every side a multiple of \(12n\) is the same triangle scaled, so each row below is one \((x, y, z)\) and the angle \(\theta\) between \(a\) and \(c\), which scaling does not change. \(\cos\theta = a/c = x/z\) is always a fraction of whole numbers.

Finding the angle in integers

Halving the reference angle needs a square root — the cosine of an eighth of a turn is \(\sqrt2/2\). Doubling the triangle's own angle does not: \(\cos 2\theta = 2\cos^2\theta - 1\). So keep \(\cos\theta = p/q\) as two integers, double the angle each step, and read one binary digit off the sign.

  1. start with \(p/q = x/z\), reduced
  2. \(p > 0\): the angle is under a quarter turn, write 0; \(p < 0\): past it, write 1
  3. new denominator \(q \cdot q\) — the cross multiply of the latest denominator
  4. new numerator \(2p^2 - q^2\), with its sign flipped when the digit was 1 (that flip takes away the half turn)
  5. divide both by their gcd, and repeat

After \(n\) digits, the digits read as a binary number \(N\) are the angle on a ring of \(2^n\) per half turn. With a quarter turn \(= 2m\), \(m = 2^{n-2}\), and the angle lies between \(N\) and \(N + 1\).

worked, \(k = 2\), \((3, 2, 5)\): \(3/5 > 0\) → 0, \((18 - 25)/25 = -7/25\); \(-7/25 < 0\) → 1, \(-(98 - 625)/625 = 527/625\); \(527/625 > 0\) → 0; …

every operation is a sign test, a multiply, a subtract or a gcd — the numbers are whole throughout

On the three-node card's ring

The card counts \(12\) to a turn and \(3\) to a quarter. Halving counts \(2^{n+1}\) to a turn. Both are whole on a ring of \(12 \cdot 2^{n-1}\): multiply every angle on the card by \(s = 2^{n-1}\), and every halving result \(N\) by \(3\).
