The equation as a count

Every term is a count of unit squares: \(a^2\) of them, plus \(2k\) blocks of \(b^2\), make \(c^2\).

\[ a^2 + 2k\,b^2 = c^2 \qquad (a, b, c) = 12n\,(x, y, z) \qquad x^2 + 2k\,y^2 = z^2 \]

Each row is one \((x, y, z)\) with no common factor; multiplying by \(12n\) gives the rest.

A direction is a pair

Fix \(k\). A direction is any pair of whole numbers \((x, y)\), not both \(0\). Putting one direction after another — turning by the first, then by the second — is one rule, called combine. It only multiplies, adds and subtracts; it never divides or takes a root.

\[ \text{combine}\big((x_1, y_1),\, (x_2, y_2)\big) = (x_1 x_2 - 2k\,y_1 y_2,\;\; x_1 y_2 + x_2 y_1) \]

In words, the new pair is:

  • first number — the two first numbers multiplied, minus \(2k\) times the two second numbers multiplied
  • second number — each first number times the other pair's second number, added together

\(\cdot\) on this page is only ever ordinary multiplication of whole numbers.

  • size — every pair has one: \(x^2 + 2k\,y^2\). The rows of the table above are the pairs whose size is a square, \(z^2\); other pairs are directions too. Combining multiplies sizes: the size of combine\((p, q)\) is the size of \(p\) times the size of \(q\)
  • multiple — \(m\) times a pair is \((m x, m y)\), for a whole number \(m > 0\)
  • same direction — \((x_1, y_1)\) and \((x_2, y_2)\) with \(x_1 y_2 = x_2 y_1\) and signs matching; a pair and any multiple of it have the same direction
  • full turn — any pair \((x, 0)\) with \(x > 0\)
  • half turn — any pair \((x, 0)\) with \(x < 0\)
  • quarter turn — \((0, 1)\), since combine\(\big((0, 1), (0, 1)\big) = (-2k, 0)\), a half turn
  • turned back — \((x, -y)\) undoes \((x, y)\), since combine\(\big((x, y), (x, -y)\big) = (x^2 + 2k\,y^2, 0)\), a full turn
  • steps — a pair is 1 step; each further step combines the result so far with the same pair once more — like taking the same turn \(j\) times
  • ring of \(R\) — a pair \(e\) whose first full turn comes at \(R\) steps; \(e\) is 1 tick, and a direction is \(j\) ticks when it has the same direction as \(j\) steps of \(e\)

For \(k = 1\) and the pair \((1, 2)\), size \(1 + 2 \cdot 4 = 9\):

  • 1 step: \((1, 2)\), size \(9\)
  • 2 steps: combine\(\big((1, 2), (1, 2)\big) = (1 \cdot 1 - 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2,\; 1 \cdot 2 + 1 \cdot 2) = (-7, 4)\), size \(49 + 32 = 81\)
  • 3 steps: combine\(\big((-7, 4), (1, 2)\big) = (-7 \cdot 1 - 2 \cdot 4 \cdot 2,\; -7 \cdot 2 + 4 \cdot 1) = (-23, -10)\), size \(529 + 200 = 729\)

The size multiplies by the same amount every step, so the numbers only get larger. The one question is whether the second number ever becomes \(0\): a half or full turn.
