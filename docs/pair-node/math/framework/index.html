\(\tau_{\varphi}\) is the whole turn on \(\varphi\), so \(\tau_{\varphi}/2\) is the far end of the line and \(\tau_{\varphi}/4\) is a right angle; at \(\tau_{\varphi} = 12\) those are \(6\) and \(3\)

Points and lines

12 points. This is what travels on the wire.
\(k\) joined to \(k+6\): 6 lines.
A node holds one line. Both ends move together.

Which end is acute decides the sign

arrival \(1\): top \(1\), bottom \(5\) — the top is acute, offset \(-1\)
arrival \(3\): top \(3\), bottom \(3\) — neither is, offset \(0\)
arrival \(5\): top \(5\), bottom \(1\) — the bottom is acute, offset \(+1\)

Top is \(0\), so bottom is \(\tau/2\). Each end keeps its own distance, and the one under \(\tau/4\) is the one that names the direction to step.

Parallel is a separation, not a place

both tops \(1\); arrival \(4\), distances \(3\) and \(3\) — offset \(0\), settled
both tops \(4\); arrival \(7\), distances \(3\) and \(3\) — offset \(0\), settled somewhere else entirely
tops \(1\) and \(2\); each sees \(5\) and \(4\), distances \(4,2\) and \(2,4\) — offsets \(+1\) and \(-1\), still turning

rotate both rings together and both distances are unchanged, so a settled pair sits anywhere on the circle

the first two are the same pair, turned — nothing in the arithmetic names a place

the third is one step from the first, and that one step is what turns

The wire carries the normal

top \(0\), sent as \(\tau/4\)

every arrival is a partner's top shifted by \(\tau/4\), which is why a parallel pair settles at distances of \(\tau/4\) rather than \(0\)

send the top instead of its normal and the shift is gone
